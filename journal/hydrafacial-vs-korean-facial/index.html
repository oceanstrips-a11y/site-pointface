The short answer

A classic Hydrafacial is a device-led protocol: hydrodermabrasion to cleanse, extract and infuse serums. A Korean Hydrafacial uses the same technology but adds a multi-step Korean skincare ritual — layered K-beauty actives, masks and massage — for a hydrated "glass skin" finish. A manual facial uses hands and natural products only: more sensory and relaxing, with gentler, shorter-lived results. For visible glow and deep cleansing in Bali's climate, the Korean Hydrafacial gives the best of both.

Key takeaways

  • Hydrafacial = device-led deep cleansing, extraction and serum infusion.
  • Korean Hydrafacial = the same technology + a layered K-beauty ritual for glass skin.
  • Manual facial = hands and natural products only: relaxing, gentle, less corrective.
  • Every POINT · FACE treatment starts with a free AI skin analysis to choose for you.

What is a Hydrafacial?

A Hydrafacial is a non-invasive facial performed with a hydrodermabrasion device. A spiral-tipped handpiece uses water and gentle suction (a "vortex") to exfoliate dead cells, loosen and extract what clogs the pores, and infuse serums at the same time. It is fast, painless and has no downtime. A classic Hydrafacial follows a set protocol: cleanse and exfoliate, extract, hydrate.

What is a Korean Hydrafacial?

A Korean Hydrafacial keeps the hydrodermabrasion technology and builds a Korean skincare ritual around it: double cleansing, essences and ampoules applied in thin layers, ultrasonic infusion, a sheet or HydroJelly mask and plenty of massage. The actives are typical of K-beauty — snail mucin, centella asiatica, propolis, niacinamide, peptides, hyaluronic acid — from brands such as COSRX, SKIN1004, Beauty of Joseon, Mediheal and Medicube. The goal is the famous glass skin: clean, plump, luminous.

At POINT · FACE, our systems also include oxygen, radiofrequency, microcurrent, high frequency, a cold hammer and LED, so each Korean Hydrafacial is tailored to a concern.

In short: a Hydrafacial is excellent for a deep clean; a Korean Hydrafacial adds the hydration, layering and massage that give the lasting glow. In a tropical climate, skin needs both — sweat, sunscreen and humidity clog pores, while sun, salt and air-conditioning dehydrate.

What is a manual facial?

A manual facial is performed with the hands and natural products only, without devices: cleansing, gentle exfoliation, extractions by hand, a mask and a long massage. Ours are Balinese — the Hydrating Ritual (Rose & Aloe) and the Purifying Ritual (Clay & Turmeric) — with botanicals from Utama Spice & Embun.

FAQ

What is the difference between a Korean Hydrafacial and a Hydrafacial?

Both use hydrodermabrasion to cleanse, extract and infuse. A Korean Hydrafacial adds a layered K-beauty ritual — essences, ampoules, masks and massage with Korean actives — for more hydration and a glass-skin glow.

Is a Korean Hydrafacial better than a manual facial?

It is more results-driven: deeper cleansing, better penetration of actives and a more visible glow. A manual facial is gentler and more sensory. Choose by your skin and your goal — or let our free skin analysis decide.

How often can I get a Korean Hydrafacial?

Every 2–4 weeks. In Bali's climate, every 3–4 weeks is a good rhythm for most skin types.
